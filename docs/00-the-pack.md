# The pack, and what it closes.

Colophon finds children’s books whose adaptation rights are available — or about to be — and routes them toward film and television. This section indexes the seven discovery deliverables and states the decisions each one is meant to close.

## 01 — The thesis

*Rights come free in three ways. Recapture is the slowest and the narrowest of them — and the only one anybody is watching.*

The music engagement built a recapture engine over a catalog its owner already controlled. Colophon has no catalog. Assembling one is the product, and the statutory clock is a single input inside a wider question: for any children’s book, who holds the motion picture and television rights right now, and what is the cheapest credible path to acquiring them.

**A · Contractual reversion**  Out-of-print, availability, and non-exploitation clauses in the publishing agreement. Children’s backlist falls out of print constantly; rights revert on request, with no statute involved. Fastest channel, largest population.

**B · Option lapse**  Most film and television options are never exercised. Rights return quietly to the author and no public record marks the moment. Highest-value channel because the property has already been validated by a buyer once.

**C · Statutory termination**  17 U.S.C. § 203 for post-1977 grants, § 304(c)/(d) for pre-1978 grants. Slow, unwaivable, and calendar-predictable — which makes it the channel you can plan a relationship around years in advance.

## 04 — Assumptions carried through

**i · Standalone venture**  Colophon is independent of the music engagement. No client catalog, no shared counterparties, no shared code until an IP review of the consulting terms says otherwise.

**ii · Operator-first**  The first user is the person working the pipeline, not a subscriber. Every screen answers “what do I do next with this book.”

**iii · Leads, not opinions**  Colophon produces evidence-backed leads with confidence tiers. It never asserts clear title and never advises a rightsholder.

**iv · US trade children’s books**  Picture books and middle grade published in the United States, 1960–1998, with the 1978–1992 grant band as the opening focus.
